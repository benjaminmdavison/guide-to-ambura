St. Alberich

The hidden village of St. Alberich is an idyllic paradise, nestled between the shores of the Pearl Sea to the southeast and the Dragonback Mountains to the northwest. At the center of the village is a fortress with two towering spires called Saint's Rest, built on the remains of the ancient fort known as Hilltop. Below the fortress, the village spirals down through the hillside, with gates and stone walls marking out distinctive neighborhoods. The walled city ends at the ancient and beautiful Saint's Gate, where legend has it that Saint Alberich fended off the devil centuries ago.

Outside of the gate, a sweeping road forks in two directions. The path to the southeast leads to a bustling harbor. The road is criss-crossed with intersections of smaller cart paths leading out through lush and verdant farmland. The harbor is small, but capable. Local fishermen ply their trade in the ocean, and small merchant vessels come and go every few weeks, allowing the locals to exchange wares with other cities along the Peppered Coast. The occasional pirate ship sails past the mouth of the harbor, but well-placed ballistae batteries called The Jaws keep them away from the harbor itself and the village behind it.

The northwest path ventures towards the Dragonback Mountains, which loom large behind the village. They are not the largest mountains in the world, but they are large enough to be forbidding to travelers who are not well-equipped for a challenging journey. The name holds ominous promise: legend has it that Ancient Dragons still fly among the peaks, though they have not been sighted by reliable witnesses in centuries. The northwest road leads several miles to a small fortification which is perched on a plateau that the locals call Boulder-Top. In times of old, Boulder-Top acted as a breakwater for marauders and beasts from the mountains who might attempt a raid on St. Alberich. Today, the fortification offers one final chance for travelers to check their kit and make ready for the difficult passage through the mountains along a road called The Narrows.

Surrounded by mountains on one flank and ocean on the other, St. Alberich is highly defensible but also somewhat isolated. It is not disconnected from the larger world, but the challenges in getting there keep it isolated from the chaos and thrum of the broader kingdom. Merchants and traders are common, but travelers, wanderers, and marauders are vanishingly rare. Merchants tend to arrive in cycles. Between visits from merchant ships, the town is fairly sleepy. In a world wracked with strife, warfare, chaos, and magic, St. Alberich is uncommonly beautiful, fertile, prosperous, and safe.

Climate and Geography

St. Alberich is the far southeastern terminus of the Norsatch Promontory. The Dragonback Mountains hug the coastline for approximately twenty miles northwestbound, making the sea in this area treacherous. The mountains then swoop west, blocking the Promontory off from the mainland except for a few challenging passages. Along the coast, sunken mountains, hidden reefs, and sudden plunges generate unusual currents, making the seaborne approach to St. Alberich rather difficult. Vessels bound for the larger cities to the north generally tack out to sea by about ten miles to avoid the waters closer to the mountains. The prevailing winds blow southeasterly, moving humidity in from the sea and up the mountainsides, generating fearsome thunderstorms, lenticular clouds, and snow in the mountains but keeping the village and farmlands rather pleasant. Babbling brooks from the mountains water the fertile lands. While torrential storms and harsh weather periodically boil out at sea, the harbor and the village tend to enjoy pleasant climes. Winters are mild in the village but deathly cold in the mountains. Summers are warm, but the sea breeze cools the air.

Between the mountains and the sea is a long, fertile bowl. St. Alberich sits in the center of this bowl, surrounded by farmlands.

Quirks

Despite the pleasant climate, St. Alberich is home to unsettling phenomena.

Usually, the night sky features the same two moons and countless stars as it does anywhere in the world. When both moons have waned or both moons are full, the sky shimmers brilliantly at night, turning uncommonly purple, pink, and green as strange lights dart through the sky. The daytime is marked with an unusual orange glow. During these times, fishermen get lost coming home and travelers lose their way on the pass. Farmers who stray too far from their lands find themselves lost in unfamiliar fields dotted with sinister trees. Some unfortunate souls report the trees speaking to them, whispering of the superiority of bark and spitting contempt for wearers of flesh. These unsettling experiences are hard to shake for visitors, but locals seem to bear them in stride; it's the only world they've ever known, and the trees don't seem to be mobile, which makes their insults rather impotent.

The strangeness continues in the town. Townsfolk lose track of small items, which often turn up in unpredictable places. Villagers are sometimes drawn to wander toward the mountains or the sea, waking from their reveries when the cold or the water shake them loose. Visitors to the town almost always report the uncanny sensation of being watched. Unusual travelers sometimes arrive from The Narrows, lightly equipped considering the perilous journey, observing the villagers with keen eyes and unusual questions before vanishing back into the mountains. Strange creatures appear in peripheral vision, only to vanish at the turn of the head. Terrible smells will blow in on the wind, followed by the intoxicating scent of summer flowers. Massive leviathans are seen in the harbor.

At odd intervals, the Saint's Gate will glow and shimmer with iridescent colors, becoming frigid to the touch or steaming with unusual heat. Brave souls who touch the gate during these happenings have equal odds of going mad or experiencing nothing at all.

Some believe that these occurrences are related to the villagers' habitual consumption of the delicious but foreboding Fairycap Mushrooms that sprout in the fields, which are known to cause a pleasant lightheadedness and strange dreams. Others believe that the phenomena are the result of ancient enchantments dating back to the founding of the village. Nobody knows for sure, and the locals endure these odd happenings with stoic amusement. Sometimes, people can't take it and leave town on a ship; other times, madmen and misfits from abroad come to make their home in St. Alberich.

Despite these quirks, about 2,500 people call St. Alberich home at any given time.

Society

St. Alberich is overseen by the Lord of the Hill, Solomon New-Dawn. Legend has it that the family is descended from the Saint himself, and they have ruled over St. Alberich since its founding. While St. Alberich has never technically been conquered or incorporated into the broader Kingdom of Breland, the Lord will occasionally send tributes and missionaries to Lord Barkus Wyrmbane, who rules the nearest terrestrial region on the other side of the Dragonback Mountains, a large hold whimsically known as The Screelands for reasons lost to history. Barkus Wyrmbane is next in line of succession, first of the king's five sons.

King Philip Wyrmbane the Fair has presided over a time of uncommon strife and corruption in the broader Kingdom of Breland. The remoteness and relatively inconsequential nature of St. Alberich leave it relatively far removed from the troubles of the kingdom at large.

The Lord maintains a retinue of about 100 soldiers, and about 200 of the locals work in Saint's Rest. Each battery in The Jaws employs 15 soldiers, leaving 70 in the castle and on the gate.

The Lord delegates most of his duties to The Council, comprised of seven prominent locals. The Council employs another 100 or so individuals who perform municipal work, including 25 constables, a harbor master, an engineer, a tax collector, street sweeps, carpenters, bricklayers, and smiths. The remainder of the residents enjoy normal occupations, such as farming, shopkeeping, toolmaking, and so on.

Apart from Lord New-Dawn, there are several prominent residents in St. Alberich. Perhaps the most widely known is the local mage, Jorius van der Smoot, who resides in one of the spires of Saint's Rest without being formally employed by the Lord. Jorius provides basic magical services for the townsfolk, occasionally disappearing on strange errands or hiring locals to perform unusual tasks.

The Harbor Master is an ancient sailor known as Gusmaro Wind-Finder. Wind-Finder sailed merchant vessels for decades, and plies his trade by supervising the operations of the harbor. He will act as a harbor pilot for merchants who are willing to pay for it, and he is training his granddaughter Almira Wind-Finder in the harbor master's trade. He resides in a fine home near the harbor. His son, Gustavo Wind-Finder, never took to the sea and instead works wood and fabric as a ship's carpenter and sailmaker.

The city engineer is a gnome known as Placius Bottlenock. He maintains the city gates and supervises construction and maintenance of municipal structures.

A wealthy merchant named Samuel Delucifene lives in a sprawling manor nestled amid the farmland between the village and the mountains. He employs five bodyguards who watch over the manor house. He is sociable but secretive. While he makes money by importing and exporting goods, it is widely believed that he has a secret source of income. He avoids attracting too much suspicion by being a magnanimous local, helping those in need and sponsoring festivals for the locals. Curiously, Delucifene and van der Smoot are often seen cavorting together, holding private conversations or visiting one another at odd hours.

The Council

Gusmaro, Delucifene, and Bottlenock are members of The Council. The other members include the local priest, Arvid Featherfall; the oldest man in the village, known simply as Frederick the Old; a farmer named Quintus Fallowfield; and an upstart young villager named Wilhelm von Deribus, who recently finished an apprenticeship at the Mare's Breath Inn and who has rocketed to popularity by being somewhat of a firebrand. He is hot of temper and quite opinionated, and recently displaced a local shopkeep named Tomas the Short on the council.

The Council answers directly to The Lord, and The Lord can replace members of The Council at will. Long tradition allows for the villagers to participate in The Council by haranguing council members to advocate for their needs. In the event that a majority of villagers petition the council to have a member removed, that member is removed and replaced.

Ultimately, The Lord controls the village. The soldiers in the keep answer to him and only him. However, with his blessing, the nobility and the peasants have worked out a system that roughly works for everyone with a minimum of friction or violence.

Notable Places

  • The Windgate Inn. Swanky, clean, French-looking. Just beyond the Saint's Gate.
  • The Weary Footman. Much more modest inn. Slightly shabby but quite cozy.
  • The Saint's Fate. A small occult shop. Sells spell components, offers basic magic services, such as charms and potions.
  • The Seaport. Warehouses, sailmakers, shipwrights, woodworkers, cartiers, barrel-makers, and so on.
  • The Forum. A large public hall. Used for all manner of public purposes. Large, open room.
  • Saint's Rest. The castle of the realm.
  • Phineas' Forge. A large iron-works. Sells all manner of metal objects: farm implements, tools, weapons, armor, industrial and ship components. Many skilled metalworkers here.
  • Temple of Ptah and Garden of Eris. A spacious temple dedicated to the creator-god Ptah, with an orchard and herb garden devoted to his mistress, Eris.
  • The Tomery. A large archive of tomes, books, papers, and tablets, dating back eras.
