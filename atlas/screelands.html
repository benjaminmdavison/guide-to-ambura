The Screelands

The Screelands is a hilly region, full of unexpected steep cliffs, unusual rock formations, and sporadic rifts in the earth, much like the Badlands. Deep inside the hills are rich deposits of iron and corundum, making The Screelands a center of industry known for high-quality metals forged by talented smiths. The largest hill, Old Temperamental, was once a volcano but now seems to be dormant.

The Screelands abuts the Golden Forest to the southwest and the Dragonback Mountains to the southeast, and is bordered to the north by the lands of Brelas' Shield. The River of Diamonds defines the western edge of the Screelands, bearing west-southwest toward Porphyrium and The Crossroads. To the east, beyond the Sodden Bogs, lies the treacherous shore of the Peppered Coast; to the west lie the floodplains of the Free City. A small port named Port Bogdeep has been established, but the road between Hell's Tooth and the port is difficult to travel and harder to maintain.

The main city in The Screelands is known as Hell's Tooth. Nestled uneasily between a deep crevasse that occasionally vents sulfurous gases, and a run of enormous hoodoos, Hell's Tooth is well-fortified by nature. Karst Keep, a modest fort, sits at the center of Hell's Tooth. Despite looking unstable and vaguely rickety, Hell's Tooth has never been breached: it is an uncommonly tough fort, if also uncommonly ugly. Barkus Wyrmbane, Lord of the Screelands, sits in the Keep and controls the land on behalf of the King.

The Screelands is home to a thriving agricultural industry: roving packs of cowboys herd cattle and sheep across the land, massive orchards and fields of grain sit atop buttes, and farms dot the landscape, growing exceptional produce in the rich volcanic soil.

Four main avenues slouch outward from the keep.

To the north, toward the Hoodoos, is a sprawling run of smallish, sod-roofed houses. The people of Hell's Tooth live modest lives, and even the prominent local citizens reside in the city's average-sized sod-roofed homes. Manor homes or ostentatious displays of wealth are seen as tacky and gauche here. The northern neighborhood is quiet, relaxed, and homey. Women spin yarn or garden in front of their homes, children play in the dirt streets, and smoke wafts up from stone chimneys. Despite the rugged landscape, Hell's Tooth feels quite cozy and homey, and many a wary adventurer has considered hanging up their helm to live out their days beneath the strange shadows of the Hoodoos.

To the south, toward the crevasse, is the main commercial district. Large warehouses, massive livestock pens, and feed barns feature prominently in the commercial district. Three large windmills grind wheat into flour and corn into feed; several small bakeries produce meat pies, rich breads, and assorted sundries. A tall stone wall runs the length of the crevasse, and a reinforced iron drawbridge connects the city to the outside.

The eastern district is known as The Din because of the incessant hammering, banging, and general mayhem of industry found here. Smithies and foundries hammer out high-quality agricultural tools: sharp ploughs, high-quality tack, durable shovels and picks, pitchforks and scythes, and horseshoes. No less important are the smithies that forge the boning knives and cleavers needed by the western district, known as the Killing District.

The Killing District is simply and accurately named. Meat pies need meat, and meat comes from livestock, who are expertly slaughtered in the Killing District by teams of professional butchers. While they live in a hard land, the people of Hell's Tooth take no special delight in cruelty, and endeavor to make the necessary slaughter as clean and painless as possible for the animals they raise. Their methods result in exceptional meats. The smokehouses and spice-rooms of The Killing District make highly sought-after hams and sausages, and the larger cuts of meat are salted and aged before being shipped or sold at market. The blood and general mess of the Killing District flows through a series of clay pipes to an ejector that pours it into the crevasse, keeping the district relatively clean.
