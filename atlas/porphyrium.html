The Free City of Porphyrium

Eastern District

Temple of Thoth: large, grassy plain with open-air Roman-style forum. Full of scholars and ruffians. Mostly parkland, some shops.

  • The Grubber: grocer / eatery
  • Guild of Argumentation: lawyer's guild.
  • Fine Fings: a tailor shop.
  • The Bolting Hare: large beer hall. Sketchy. Shares space with the Sleeping Hare.

The Den

Shady, commercial. Anything you want...for a price.

  • Opium dens
  • The Granite Shore: shady bar, dark, dim. Many questionable characters.
  • Refined Chaps Home of Respite: brothel. Classy but dangerous.
  • Grim's: pawn shop / general store.

Bookgrove

  • Jacob's Tower: three-story bookshop, full of magical tomes, spellsheets, enchanted items.
  • Thundering Press: printing shop, makes posters, tomes, whatever.
  • Guild of the Shimmering Triangle: wizarding guild.
  • Enclave Meeting Hall: sorceror's enclave.
  • Nightshade and Nocturne: suspicious herbalist. Shop feels...off. Distinct feeling of being watched.
  • The Tipsy Magus: magic-users bar. Chaotic. Drinking contests lead to all kinds of shenanigans.
  • Morkus' Magical Mysteries: magic shop. Largely junk, but some good stuff. A few cursed items.
  • Silver Dragon's Guild.

The Western District

Fort Silt. Warehouses at all piers. Caravan stops at Boot Brothers Fine Wares, on Pier 3.

  • Pier 1: King's navy, off limits.
  • Pier 2: fishermen. Chum, bait, seagulls, nets, and so on.
  • Pier 3: freight. Boot Brothers Fine Wares. Several other warehouses. The Seadream Chaser is moored here.
  • Pier 4: general. A few small private sailing ships.
  • Several boathouses, boatbuilders, sailmakers.

Shoreditch

More warehouses.

  • The Wriggling Eel: large bar and inn. Pleasant enough for a big city place. Cleanish, good vibes in general.
  • Shoreditch Finest: cheap, shabby inn. Many fights. Cheap beer. Sailors, dangerous.
  • The Captain's Quarters: upscale hotel, private security.
  • Rapscallions: brothel.
  • The Crusty Seaman: poorly-named general goods shop focusing on nautical wares. Black-market connections.
  • The Popped Cork: upscale bar.
  • The Dropped Anchor: downscale bar.

The Boring Quarter

Mostly residential. Heavily patrolled by local guards.

Continue west on the River of Diamonds, across the floodplains, and you come to the Free City of Porphyrium. Porphyrium sits astride an ever-shifting network of small rivers, wetlands, and floodplains, anchored to the enormous, muddy River Twain, which runs southwest to the sea from the Borovian Mountains.

Technically not free, this city is in fact ruled by the fourth son of King Phillip, Lord Geoffrey Wyrmbane, the Strong. The Free City has made overtures of secession in the past, and proudly pretends not to be part of the kingdom, but Geoff maintains order in the fashion expected of him by the King.

The Eastern District of the Free City is a heady, paradoxical mix of scholarly types and dangerous ruffians, making it an ideal place to go for those seeking adventure, chaos, or self-destruction. The center of the Eastern District is the Temple of Thoth, a large, grassy parkland featuring a massive temple dedicated to the Ibis-headed god of knowledge. The Temple is a place where anybody can learn anything, as long as someone else is there to teach it; worshipers of Thoth believe in the free and unimpaired exchange of knowledge.

Inland of the Temple is a busy urban neighborhood known as The Den, which bustles with commerce and dens dedicated to the endless distractions of mortals. Broad Way, the main drag of The Den, is full of colorful and dangerous characters who can get you anything you want…at a price.

Riverward of the Temple is a more organized neighborhood known as Bookgrove, known for its abundance of two-storied towers belonging to wordsmiths, enchanters, scholars, and artificers. Low-slung stone buildings housing libraries, bookbinders, printers, and scribes round out the district.

There are three ways to cross the River Twain to the Western District. Ferries of all sizes and shapes can be found along the riverbank, and by far the most popular method of crossing the river is to hire one of these. If you have a taste for danger, you can try your luck on the bridges.

The first bridge is a terrifying floating bridge known as The Python. It is comprised of two halves, each of which is itself comprised of many smallish, pontoon-mounted wooden wharves of about twenty feet in length. Each section slopes gradually upward, until the halves meet at about 30 feet above the water at a section called The Bridge House. The Bridge House is anchored to the floor of the river by huge stone pylons that form parallel arches about 30 feet high and 50 feet wide, through which smaller vessels can sail. Each half of The Python is lashed to The Bridge House with thick ropes. Either half can be released from The Bridge House to let larger vessels pass through the river, but releasing one half of The Python causes the whole half-bridge to ride the current down the river, which is disorienting and unpleasant for those currently on the span. The Bridgemen generally sound a warning, but it’s not always adequate for those on the distant ends of the bridge, and many locals have found themselves trying to balance on a half-bridge as it swings wide and floats downstream.

The other bridge is known as The Spire. The Spire rises gracefully above the river to a dazzling height of 100 feet, and is supported by a series of impossibly slender wooden supports that hold a thin stone bridge aloft between them. The ancient builders of The Spire apparently believed that safety was for the weak. The central span of the bridge features no guardrails of any kind. The stone surface can become quite slick, and countless locals have plunged to an untimely death after planting a careless footfall on the bridge. The Spire is about fifteen feet wide and offers beautiful views of the land from its apex, but those afraid of heights or death should avoid The Spire at all costs.

The Western District features a large fortress, Fort Silt, that sits at the riverbank between the bridges. Geoff Wyrmbane rules over the land from Fort Silt, and a detachment of soldiers patrol the parapets of the fort. A portcullis closes off a small boat pen that houses three fast riverboats, which Lord Wyrmbane’s men use to patrol the waters. North and South of the fort are modest-sized piers, which house riverboats and smaller oceangoing vessels.

Radiating back from the piers is a rowdy neighborhood known as Shoreditch. Like the rest of the Free City, Shoreditch is a mashup of life. Wealthy merchants, sea captains, and shipwrights hobnob with military officers and influential locals at fine restaurants and bespoke inns, drinking fine wines and eating delicate cuisine. Across the street, sailors, dockworkers, soldiers, and other rabble drink rotgut whiskey at seedy bars, patronize Ladies or Gentlemen of Negotiable Affection, and slouch about doing nefarious things.

Behind Shoreditch is a quieter neighborhood known as The Boring Quarter. True to name, The Boring Quarter is mostly residential, with a few warehouses and business offices scattered about. The Boring Quarter ends at a rough wooden palisade, and beyond that, the Sea of Grass. The Sea of Grass is deceptively dangerous. With the notable exception of Bramblewood, it carries on for leagues and leagues before meeting the ocean on an unruly, grassy beach.

Cosmopolitan, worldly, and occasionally seedy, The Free City is a place where people often go to seek their fortune and just as often fail to find it. You might connect with someone influential and learned; you might make a fortune; or you might wake up hungover and indentured on a merchant ship destined for the Southlands. Adventure awaits in the Free City of Porphyrium.
